THE RUST TILE

Five ways to draw the table's second element with no emoji. Each is shown at the three sizes the game renders .s at, then dropped in beside the warm swatches it has to stay distinct from. Costs are measured builds against a 12685 B baseline — 157 elements, 627 B of headroom.

THE CROWDED WARM END

The real constraint, in one row. Brown and Orange are flat fills; Sand is mottled tan. Charcoal and Ash have grey faces — but Charcoal glows #8a3a14, an ember hue a rust swatch would fight for. Metal is on the end because Rust comes from it, and looking unlike its own parent is the one thing this swatch must not do.

01

THE NUT AND BOLT

+11 B measured

U+1F529, the closest emoji Unicode offers, and the cheapest thing on this page — cheaper even than a flat colour, which is worth a pause. It is also a picture of clean hardware. Nothing about it is corroded.

  • Cheapest by 5 B, and legible at 32px — emoji are drawn for it.
  • Costs less than option 02 because an emoji escape shares its shape with dozens already in the table, while #b7410e is a brand-new seven-character string roadroller has never seen.
  • It depicts a fastener, not corrosion. A player reads "Bolt".
  • No glow — the c/bg branch precedes el.e, so it sits dark between Metal and Charcoal, which both glow.
  • Says nothing about Metal, its parent.
02

THE FLAT FILL

+16 B measured

One hex, c:"#b7410e", no bg — the Brown and Orange treatment. The cheapest actual swatch, and the cheapest option that glows.

  • Glows, and in the right hue.
  • Precedent is the oldest in the table — the pure colours are exactly this.
  • Perfectly scale-independent.
  • It is a plain orange-brown square in a table that already has Brown and Orange as plain squares. At 32px it is a third one.
  • Rust is a texture. A flat fill is the one thing rust is not.
03

CORROSION ON STEEL

+61 B measured

Rust blooms eating into a cool steel ground. The only option in which two materials are visible at once — which is what rust actually is, and what makes it unmistakably a child of Metal. Chosen. What is left is how far the corrosion has got, in three stages below.

THE INTENSITY IS FREE. All three were built and measured: +61 B, +63 B, +60 B. A gradient stop costs the same whatever colour it names, and the heaviest version is the cheapest of the three by a byte. So this is a pure design choice with no budget argument on either side — pick the one that looks right.

03a  EARLY — steel dominant, the original +61 B
03b  ADVANCED — four blooms grown until steel is only the gaps +63 B
03c  HEAVY — the ground itself is rust; steel survives as one band +60 B
  • Unlike every neighbour by construction: nothing else in the table shows one material consuming another.
  • Visibly descends from Metal — the grey is Metal's own ramp, so the ancestry reads without a caption.
  • Reads as rust at 32px, where the blooms are still soft blobs rather than detail.
  • Glows warm against Metal's cool tile — the pair sit well together.
  • ~60 B whichever stage wins: roughly 10% of the remaining headroom, and four times the flat fill.
  • 03a was too shy. At 32px the blooms read as three marks on a steel plate rather than as corrosion, and the tile is grey first, orange second — so it sat closer to Metal than to anything warm.
  • 03c inverts that and makes the tile read orange first. The steel band has to stay: without it this collapses into option 04 at twice the price, since the surviving metal is the whole idea.
04

MOTTLED RUST

+38 B measured

The Sand and Charcoal idiom applied straight: two speckles over a diagonal rust ramp. Structurally it is the table's house style for a rough material — which is its strength and its problem.

  • 23 B cheaper than option 03 and still textured, not flat.
  • Uses an idiom the table has already established three times.
  • Being the house style is the trap: at 32px it is one more speckled warm square in a row that already has Sand, and it glows the same ember family as Charcoal.
  • Shows no metal, so nothing connects it to its parent.
  • The strongest case for it is budget: if 03 is unaffordable later, this is what it degrades to.
05

THE CORRODED PLATE

+112 B measured

Inline SVG on the 0 0 32 32 viewBox, the matter route: a steel plate with rust eating across it and two pits. The most literal, and by a distance the most expensive thing on this page.

  • Unambiguous at 110px, and genuinely sharp as the hero.
  • Only option that reads as an object that has rusted.
  • +112 B — 18% of the remaining headroom for one tile.
  • At 32px the pits vanish and the ragged edge reads as noise; it becomes a worse version of option 03 at four times the size.
  • A rectangular plate inside a rounded 32px square wastes the corners, so it looks smaller than every swatch beside it.

WHERE THIS COMES OUT

Option 03 is chosen — for the same reason the pool won the Oasis: it is the only one whose picture is the definition. Rust is not a colour, it is steel being eaten, and 03 is the only option where both materials are present. It inherits Metal's ground, so the parent is legible at a glance — and in a warm end of the palette that already holds Brown, Orange, Sand and Charcoal's ember glow, being the only two-material swatch is the cheapest distinctiveness available.

Of the three stages, take 03c. 03a was too shy: at 32px it reads grey first and orange second, which puts it nearer Metal than to anything that looks corroded. 03c inverts the ratio so the tile is orange first while one band of bare steel survives — and that band is not decoration, it is the entire argument for option 03 over option 04, so it must not be dropped. 03b is the honest middle if 03c reads as too far gone. All three cost the same within 3 B, so nothing but the look is at stake.

Option 04 is the fallback if ~60 B is too rich; it keeps the texture and loses the meaning. Option 02 is honest but forgettable. Option 01 is the wrong picture, and option 05 loses at the size it is mostly seen — the same verdict the SVG got in the Oasis experiment, for the same reason.
