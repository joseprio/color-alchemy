THE OIL TILE

Five ways to draw the table's third element with no emoji — and the first that has to survive a dark end of the palette that is already full. Each is shown at the three sizes the game renders .s at, then dropped in beside the three near-black swatches it must not echo. Costs are measured builds against a 12821 B baseline.

1-5 jump to an option  ·  C toggle notes

THREE DARK SWATCHES ALREADY

The real constraint. Black, Charcoal and Night are all near-black, and each bought its legibility differently: Black with a sheen blob and a grey glow, Charcoal with a faceted slab and an ember glow, Night with stars and a violet glow. Black's own comment in elements.ts says why — a plain black square vanishes into the tile. A fourth dark swatch has to earn its place the same way.

01

THE OIL DRUM

+10 B measured

U+1F6E2, the only emoji in the neighbourhood, and the cheapest thing here. It is a picture of the barrel — the container oil is shipped in, not the substance. Rust had exactly this problem with the nut and bolt.

  • Cheapest, by 2 B, and needs no design decision to maintain.
  • Depicts the container, not the contents. A player reads "Barrel".
  • The worst no-glow case in the game. Emoji cannot carry --g, and this one is a dark object on a dark tile between three neighbours that all glow. It is the only option that gets harder to see because of where it sits.
  • Industrial, where every other route to Oil is geological.
02

THE FLAT FILL

+12 B measured

One hex, c:"#1c1c24", no bg. Cheap, and it does glow — but it walks straight into the trap Black documented and then avoided.

  • Second cheapest, and unlike option 01 it carries a glow.
  • It nearly vanishes. The tile ground is #0c1122; a #1c1c24 face is barely a shade off it, so the swatch reads as a hole rather than an object.
  • A dark flat fill also makes the glow do all the work, and a dark glow is a dim one.
  • Black solved this exact problem with a sheen blob and a deliberately GREY glow. Any flat-dark option has to do the same, at which point it is no longer flat.
03

THE GLOSSY POOL

+47 B measured

Black made wet: the same dark ramp, but with a broad specular highlight that reads as a liquid surface rather than a solid. The cheapest option that is unmistakably not one of the three incumbents.

  • Says LIQUID, which is the one thing Black, Charcoal and Night all fail to say — they are a colour, a solid and a sky.
  • Survives at 32px: a big soft highlight is the shape that scales down best.
  • 14 B cheaper than the slick, and much cheaper than the SVG.
  • Its kinship with Black is a little too close. At tile size it reads as "Black, but shinier", which is a weak identity for an element with its own recipe.
  • The honest budget pick if the slick is too rich.
04

THE IRIDESCENT SLICK

+75 B measured

A petrol rainbow floating on black — thin-film interference, the one thing oil looks like and nothing else in this table does. The spectrum uses the game's own six hues, so it belongs to the palette rather than importing a new one.

  • Unique by construction. The game owns a spectrum — Prism draws one, Rainbow is an emoji — but neither puts one ON black, and a spectrum on black is exactly what a slick is.
  • Solves the dark-end problem instead of fighting it: the blackness is the ground the colour sits on, so being dark is now an asset rather than a collision.
  • Glows petrol-teal (#3fa88f), a hue no neighbour uses — Black is grey, Charcoal ember, Night violet.
  • Reads at 32px, because the bands are wide and soft.
  • +75 B, about 15% of the remaining headroom, and second most expensive on the page.
  • Bands are held at 55 alpha on purpose. Push them brighter and it stops being oil and becomes Rainbow with the lights off.
05

THE DROP

+89 B measured

Inline SVG on the 0 0 32 32 viewBox: a single black droplet with a highlight and a slick pooling under it. The most literal, the most expensive, and the option that fits the 32px square worst.

  • Unambiguous at 110px — a drop is a drop.
  • The only option that reads as a discrete OBJECT rather than a material sample.
  • +89 B, the dearest here.
  • A drop is tall and narrow; it leaves the rounded square's corners empty, so it looks smaller than every swatch beside it — the same fault the Rust plate had for the opposite reason.
  • At 32px the highlight and the pool both disappear, leaving a dark blob on a dark tile, which is option 01 again at nine times the price.

WHERE THIS COMES OUT

Option 04, the iridescent slick. It is the only one that turns the problem into the answer: three neighbours are already dark, so being dark is not a way to stand out — but being dark with a spectrum on it is, and it happens to be precisely what oil looks like. It also takes a glow hue nothing else in the table uses, which is the cheapest legibility available at the dark end.

Note this breaks the pattern of the previous two experiments: for the Oasis and for Rust the winning option was the middle of the price range. Here it is second-dearest, because the cheap options are not merely duller — they are actively invisible. Option 01 is a dark emoji with no glow on a dark tile, and option 02 is a shade off the tile ground. Option 03 is the real fallback at 28 B less: it says "liquid", which is enough, but it reads as Black with a shine. Option 05 loses at 32px, the same way the SVG lost in both earlier experiments.
